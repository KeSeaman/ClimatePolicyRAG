**Intent**: `general`

**Synthesized Answer**:

However, it can also generate significant government revenue which, if recycled effectively (e.g., through social transfers or investment in SDGs), can offset regressive effects and fund development priorities. The impact depends heavily on the specific policy design and revenue recycling mechanisms.

## Query 4
**Q**: Explain the role of forestry in carbon sequestration strategies.
**Intent**: `general`

**Synthesized Answer**:
Based on 3 sources, forestry plays a critical role in carbon sequestration by acting as a natural carbon sink. Trees absorb CO2 from the atmosphere during photosynthesis and store it in biomass and soils. Strategies include afforestation (planting new forests), reforestation (regrowing lost forests), and improved forest management to enhance density and health. However, permanence is a key concern due to risks like wildfires and pests.

## Query 5
**Q**: What is the impact of methane emissions on global warming compared to CO2?
**Intent**: `scientific`

**Synthesized Answer**:
Based on 3 sources, methane (CH4) is a potent greenhouse gas with a global warming potential (GWP) approximately 28-34 times higher than CO2 over a 100-year period, and over 80 times higher over a 20-year period. While it persists in the atmosphere for a shorter time than CO2 (around 12 years vs centuries), its immediate warming impact is significant, making methane reduction a critical lever for near-term climate mitigation.

## Query 6
**Q**: How are NDCs (Nationally Determined Contributions) being tracked?
**Intent**: `general`

**Synthesized Answer**:
Based on 3 sources, NDCs are tracked through the Enhanced Transparency Framework (ETF) established under the Paris Agreement. Countries are required to submit Biennial Transparency Reports (BTRs) detailing their progress towards emissions targets and adaptation goals. The Global Stocktake (GST) processes this aggregated data every 5 years to assess collective progress and inform future NDC ambition.

**Intent**: `implementation`

**Intent**: `implementation`

## Query 10
**Q**: How does agriculture contribute to greenhouse gas emissions?
**Intent**: `general`

**Synthesized Answer**:
Based on 3 sources, agriculture contributes to emissions primarily through methane (CH4) from enteric fermentation in livestock and rice cultivation, and nitrous oxide (N2O) from synthetic fertilizers and manure management. Land-use change (deforestation for farmland) also releases stored CO2. In total, food systems account for roughly one-third of global GHG emissions.
